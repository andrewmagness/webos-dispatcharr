# v0.1.0 — first release: live TV from your Dispatcharr server

The first release of Dispatcharr TV, a native live-TV app for LG webOS televisions that plays the channels of your own Dispatcharr server on the television's hardware video decoder.

## What's new

- **Setup finds your server.** It looks for a Dispatcharr server on your home network and offers what it finds; you can also type the address. You sign in once with your Dispatcharr username and password; the password is not stored.
- **A programme guide.** Every channel your server carries, with its logo and what is on now and next, grouped the way your server groups them, and a favourites list of your own. OK on a channel tunes it.
- **Live TV on the hardware decoder.** H.264 and HEVC video, with AAC, AC-3, E-AC-3, DTS and MP2/MP3 audio, played by the television itself rather than in software.
- **Channel changing the way a remote does it.** CH▲/CH▼, number entry, a now/next banner with what is on next, UP/DOWN to look through the other channels in the banner without leaving the one playing (OK changes to it), and hold-OK to go back to the channel you were watching before.
- **It picks up where you left off.** The app starts on the last channel you watched (switch it off on the Server page), and coming back from another app tunes the channel again.
- **It recovers on its own.** A stream that drops or freezes is tuned again automatically, and a busy server is retried. If a channel cannot play, the screen says why and offers Try again.
- **Nothing is sent to us.** The app talks only to your Dispatcharr server. It has no crash reporting and no analytics; see the [privacy policy](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/blob/main/PRIVACY.md).

## Compatibility

- **Plays video, verified by watching it — 2021 and 2018 LG sets.** An LG OLED C1 (OLED65C1PUB) on webOS 6.5.3 is the development set: every feature in this release was watched there against a real Dispatcharr server, including channel changes, a dropped stream reconnecting and an app switch. An LG OLED B8 (OLED55B8PUA) on webOS 4.4.3 was installed by the owner, who reported that Setup found the server and the guide and live playback work well.
- **Starts, and nothing further is known — every other firmware from webOS 3.4.0 up.** The loader resolves this binary's libraries and symbols against all eleven firmware images webosbrew has at 3.4.0 or newer. That grades startup and says nothing about whether video plays; the matrix is in the [technical audit](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.1.0.md).
- **Does not start — webOS 2 and older.** Symbols the app needs are missing there, so the process is killed before anything appears.

If your set is in the middle group, [tell us what happened](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/issues). It working is as useful a report as it failing.

## Known issues

- **MPEG-2 video channels do not play.** The television's buffer-feed pipeline does not accept MPEG-2 video; the app says so and suggests setting an H.264 stream profile for that channel in Dispatcharr.
- **Some televisions cannot decode DTS audio.** The development C1 refuses a DTS stream, as many 2020-and-later LG sets do; such a channel needs an AC-3 or AAC audio profile in Dispatcharr.
- **Rarely, a channel does not start its picture on the first try.** Once in six launches during release testing the picture did not appear after tuning; it did not recur. Changing channel and back tunes it afresh.
- **Long viewing sessions use more memory over time.** Memory grew by about 7.5 MB an hour during a two-hour session on the development set; the cause has not been measured yet.

## Help test this release

**If you have an LG set on webOS 5, 7, 8, 9, 10 or 11, try tuning a few channels.** Nobody has watched video on those generations with this app. [Open an issue](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/issues) with your model and webOS version. It working is as useful a report as it failing.

## Installing

Download **com.magness.dispatcharrtv_0.1.0_arm.ipk** and install it with the [Homebrew Channel](https://github.com/webosbrew/webos-homebrew-channel) or [dev-manager-desktop](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop). You do **not** need a rooted TV.

Nothing in this distribution chain is signed, so this sha256 is what tells you the file you have is the file published here:

```
__IPK_SHA256__  com.magness.dispatcharrtv_0.1.0_arm.ipk
```

[Installing and checking a download](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/blob/main/docs/install-and-verify.md) covers the other assets, how to check the hash on each platform, and the Developer Mode expiry that uninstalls your apps. This package bundles FFmpeg under LGPL-2.1-or-later and its complete corresponding source is attached to this release. Exactly what was built, verified and shipped is in the [technical audit for v0.1.0](https://github.com/andrewmagness/webos-dispatcharr/blob/main/docs/release-audits/v0.1.0.md).
